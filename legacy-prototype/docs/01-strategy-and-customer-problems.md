# Strategy: caring for a Moroccan home from abroad

Last updated: **3 October 2026**.

## Customer and underlying problem

Proposed customer: Moroccan diaspora households with an owned apartment, family home, unoccupied second residence or inherited property in Morocco. Their distance creates an information gap: they may not know whether the property is dry, safe, secure, insured, well maintained or being used as agreed. To resolve a problem, they often need somebody local who can be trusted to verify conditions, supervise service providers and report expenses.

**This problem statement is a startup hypothesis, not a claim established by representative consumer research.** Validate by interviewing actual owners across locations and tenancy conditions.

### Seven opportunity spaces (compare by actual field evidence)

1. **Vacant-home oversight:** weather-related damage, water leaks, ventilation, keys, intrusion concerns; scheduled independent inspections, time-stamped evidence.
2. **Repair authorization and verification:** out-of-sight work, unclear price/quality; bids, approval limits, before/after proof and completion acceptance.
3. **Utilities / bills / paperwork:** forgotten payments, confusing local documents; reminders and a transaction audit ledger, without holding clients' bank credentials.
4. **Rental management:** tenants, vacancy, rent collection, repair coordination; more legal and on-site burden, longer response times.
5. **Inheritance / co-ownership coordination:** relatives may disagree on expenses and permissions; shared records, legal authority checks and consent protocols.
6. **Travel-preparation service:** owners returning in summer want a property ready; pre-arrival cleaning, readiness checklist, optional minor work.
7. **Property search / remote purchase:** trust in real estate listings and developer promises; potential due diligence checks and independent inspections, but larger liability and regulatory exposure.

## Suggested launch wedge

**Verified property care from abroad**: start with (A) one subscription for recurring checkups and (B) chargeable, documented on-demand services. Build the smallest possible physical operations footprint, **one city**, then expand only when quality and unit economics are repeatable.

A digital dashboard alone does not solve the problem. **Reliable local field execution is the product.** The interface is its accountable record: inspection checklist, source photo/video, quote, owner approval, accepted scope, verified completion, receipt and history.

## Important operating constraints

- **Independent evidence:** prohibit self-attestation by the same person who is charging for a repair, or require secondary review.
- **Money control:** explicit owner approvals and thresholds; separate recurring fee from pass-through third-party expenses; reconcile receipts.
- **Local density:** route inspectable homes by zone and day; distance matters more than app usage.
- **Language and trust:** diaspora owners may prefer French, Arabic, Spanish, Dutch or German; real humans accessible on WhatsApp/phone.
- **Scope and authority:** get the owner's authorization and key-access permissions; inherited/shared homes need all required signoffs.
- **Risk events:** leaks and intrusion need 24/7 escalation rules and referral to appropriate emergency services, not marketing claims of coverage.

## Do not confuse adjacent markets

A vacant-home inspection service is not a general property-listing website. An agent selling a home, a concierge welcoming guests, a property manager collecting rent and an independent inspector have different liabilities, margins and incentives. Trying to launch all at once would make it difficult to learn which problem customers really pay to solve.

## Critical hypotheses / failure conditions

- **Demand:** owners with existing trusted relatives may not buy an independent subscription.
- **Payment:** costs can be too high for frequent in-person visits in low-density areas.
- **Trust:** customers will not accept vague 'proof' or unqualified contractors.
- **Supply:** the time needed to recruit reliable local agents can prevent expansion.
- **Seasonality:** inspections or pre-arrival preparation may peak before summer.
- **Acquisition:** Facebook/WhatsApp diaspora communities can be accessible but CAC and trust may vary widely by origin city.

Success metric for first 90 days: number of *paid* homes with an on-time verified inspection and documented owner feedback — not site visits, emails, or registrations.
